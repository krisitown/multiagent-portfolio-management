# Inventory, contracts and financial arithmetic

## Signed positions and ownership

The position sign has economic meaning under the supplied broker convention. In a convention where positive quantity is long and negative quantity is short, a +2 put position is two owned puts, not a two-contract obligation to buy shares on assignment. A long put holder has an exercise right; the short writer has the corresponding obligation under the contract. A negative signed market value or basis can be normal for a short liability. Never infer direction solely from a profit sign, a strategy nickname or a prose description when the original quantity establishes it.

An order's effect depends on the existing position. Selling one contract against a long position of one identical contract closes that long; only an excess sale creates a short under ordinary net-position treatment. Buying against a short closes it. A proposal, hypothetical assignment or calendar event is not a completed trade. A snapshot describes the recorded inventory at its date; a later claim that it still holds requires appropriate support or a clear retained-snapshot assumption. No new account access can be inferred from a missing update.

Contract counts have multiple legitimate meanings: net signed quantity, total absolute contracts and number of rows. None alone is risk. An author saying “net exposure is three contracts” is not contradicted by six gross contracts unless it confuses the measures. For n contracts with s deliverable shares each, n × s is a share quantity, not a contract count; any cash deliverable is a separate component. Preserve contract, share, per-unit-price and aggregate-dollar labels in explanatory sentences as carefully as in tables. A long and a short on different strikes or dates are not the same contract simply because their ticker and right match.

## Identity and allocation

Relevant terms include underlying and class, right, strike, exact expiration, multiplier, deliverable, settlement method and currency; exercise style matters when discussing early exercise. Standard US equity contracts commonly cover 100 shares, but adjusted deliverables can differ [M6]. A price multiplier and a deliverable are distinct concepts. If an adjustment is unresolved, do not force standard-share funding or payoff formulas on it. Known fields in private evidence remain known even if a public response omits them. An unknown contract is not automatically invalid; it is insufficiently specified for the affected calculation.

Positions can be grouped analytically into compatible structures without proving that they were opened as those trades. Same-expiry, same-underlying, compatible-deliverable quantities can support a vertical-spread calculation under the stated grouping. A different expiry creates a calendar/diagonal problem with interim values and surviving positions; it is not the same single terminal vertical payoff. Unequal quantities leave residual exposure. An allocated contract cannot simultaneously serve a second spread, a standalone option and a hedge in an additive portfolio total. Alternate scenarios can reuse an asset as mutually exclusive comparisons if clearly labeled; double counting occurs when those scenarios are added as if simultaneous.

At an intermediate date, a later-expiring option remains alive, and assigned/exercised stock can remain after an option ends. A terminal payoff at one date cannot silently erase those surviving exposures. Settlement and exercise do not always coordinate automatically across legs. These are reasons to qualify a claim about funding or exposure, not a mandate to dismantle every spread.

## Basis, marks and cash flows

Use the actual supplied field definitions. If the record defines signed aggregate market value V and signed book basis B on the same position and currency, unrealized book P&L is V − B. For a short option with V = −180 and B = −260, the unrealized book gain is +80. If the source instead reports unsigned costs or a per-unit price, those values require their own interpretation before summation. Do not impose a convention the data does not establish.

Book basis can contain commissions, adjustments, transfers or allocation conventions. It is not independently verified historical cash premium unless the transaction record establishes that. A book-based debit and profit calculation can still be useful when labeled as such. Missing full trade history does not erase all supported mark-minus-basis arithmetic, but it prevents inventing a realized history. A roll consists of closing the old contract and opening another: realized results, new cash flow and remaining risk are separate. A new premium does not reverse an old realized loss simply because both appear in a “roll.”

For compatible standard cash terms, quantity q, multiplier m and a per-unit option price p give gross value |q|mp; an already aggregated value must not be scaled again. Aggregate signed position values and bases must refer to the same allocation and snapshot. FX translation requires the currency pair, direction, rate and date. A cash balance and its translated reporting-currency equivalent are the same resource, not two assets.

Book-adjusted acquisition arithmetic for standard short puts can be expressed conditionally. With n contracts, m shares each, strike K and a positive verified credit C, aggregate strike payment is nmK and credit-adjusted amount is nmK − C. Per share it is K − C/(nm). If C is merely the negative of signed book basis, call the result book-basis-adjusted, not an established lifetime cash cost. Taxes, fees, prior rolls and financing require their own data. These calculations do not establish margin, affordability, assignment probability or a recommendation to accept assignment.

## Arithmetic as evidence

A correct calculation on wrong operands remains a false financial conclusion. Multipliers, quantity scope, signs, allocation, date and price side are part of the calculation. Show the decisive operands in feedback so the author can repair the claim. A tool-computed number or well-formatted table does not validate its input mapping. Conversely, a clearly labeled conditional calculation with internally consistent assumptions can be correct even when those assumptions are not verified live observations. The distinction belongs in the finding's explanation, not in an invented current answer.
