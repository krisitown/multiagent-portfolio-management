# Option value, payoff, profit and events

## Payoff is not profit

For standard compatible units at expiration, a long call's intrinsic payoff is max(S − K, 0) and a long put's is max(K − S, 0), before quantity and multiplier. A short has the opposite signed payoff. These terminal functions do not give pre-expiry liquidation value or realized strategy profit. An out-of-the-money option can still have time value before expiry. A premium does not reveal the underlying's historical price: time, volatility, rates, distributions and other terms also influence value.

A standard equal-quantity same-expiry debit call vertical holds the lower-strike call and shorts the higher-strike call. A debit put vertical holds the higher-strike put and shorts the lower-strike put. For width W, quantity n, multiplier m and aggregate net debit D, the maximum expiration intrinsic payoff is nmW; the maximum expiration profit on that isolated structure is nmW − D before costs. Maximum terminal loss is D under those same assumptions. Where D is book basis, label the result book-based. The OIC debit-spread pages support this distinction [M1, M2]. Do not apply it to incompatible deliverables, mismatched dates, unallocated quantities or an entire account without additional analysis.

The short leg reduces the opening debit and caps the long option's benefit over part of the payoff range. In a compatible put debit spread, the long higher-strike put supplies the downside right; describing the short lower-strike put as the protective asset reverses the relationship. Terminal bounded payoff does not mean every interim cash obligation, exercise event or broker margin treatment is bounded by the same amount. A cash credit from an adjustment is not automatically a profit, and a spread's price can differ from its eventual intrinsic payoff.

For an isolated position held unchanged to expiration with no intervening cash flows, signed expiration P&L is signed payoff plus net opening cash flow, before costs; a received credit is positive and a paid debit negative. If signed gross payoff P is negative, its positive loss magnitude is −P: reporting that magnitude as a positive intrinsic payoff reverses the economic direction. The same sign-versus-magnitude distinction applies to net P&L after the opening cash flow. Omitting a received credit understates signed P&L while overstating a loss, if the result remains a loss. Headings, equations and proposed wording must preserve both the sign and the gross/net label. A spread's payoff sums its allocated legs; calling that total the short leg's payoff changes its scope. A correct numeral does not cure a reversed sign or mislabeled object.

## Position sensitivity and terminal protection

For stock and vanilla options on the same underlying, share-equivalent position delta is Q + sum(q_i × m_i × delta_i), where Q is signed stock quantity, q_i signed option contracts, m_i the applicable price multiplier and delta_i the per-unit long-option delta. Short quantities reverse the sign. An already position-scaled Greek must not be multiplied again; share deltas for different underlyings do not cancel merely because the numbers sum to zero [M4, M10].

A vanilla long put normally has delta between −1 and 0. An at-the-money put is often approximated near −0.5, not −1, but moneyness alone does not supply its current Greek; model inputs and conventions matter [M4, M12]. For Q positive shares covered by matching puts with common delta d, the combined delta is Q(1 + d). Equal share coverage therefore does not by itself establish delta neutrality. Missing delta leaves the exact sensitivity unresolved; it does not erase known stock ownership or the put's exercise right.

Under compatible contract terms, holding the stock and matching put to its expiry gives terminal value Q × max(S_T, K), before acquisition costs, premium and other costs. This floor at that horizon preserves upside and is distinct from today's local delta, absence of stock, or immunity to all risks [M9]. Even an explicitly supported near-zero delta is local and can change as inputs change. Other Greeks describe different sensitivities; aggregation requires matching units and definitions, not a strategy label.

## Executable-side illustrations

A holder ordinarily sells an option to close; the bid side is the natural gross-proceeds reference. A writer buys to close; the ask side is the natural gross-debit reference. A midpoint or negotiated limit price can be an explicitly hypothetical alternative. Observed prices are not guaranteed fills, even on the correct side. Quote time, delay, size, venue, crossed/stale data, spreads and multi-leg execution matter [M5]. Treat a requested limit as a request, not a completed execution.

For closing several longs, indicative proceeds are the sum of quantity × multiplier × matching bid, assuming standard scaling and adequate relevant quotes. For closing shorts, the comparable indicative debit uses asks. Mixing the mark on one expiry with the quote on another produces neither a coherent liquidation value nor a coherent book valuation. Bid/ask illustrations need not erase an independently labeled historical mark calculation. A zero or missing bid has a different meaning from a missing response; unsupported extrapolation to all expiries is a separate defect.

Liquidity is a property of a proposed transaction in a specific market context. Open interest, underlying volume, issuer size and a displayed midpoint do not establish executable options size. An observed wide market can support a qualified execution concern. Missing observations support uncertainty, not categorical liquidity or illiquidity. A hypothetical negotiated fill is allowed when identified, but cannot launder contrary observed evidence into a claim that a transaction is currently practical.

## Exercise, assignment and probability

For American-style options, exercise can occur before expiration; assignment follows exercise and the applicable allocation procedures. Being in the money now is not proof of imminent early assignment or a near-certain terminal outcome. Time to expiry, remaining extrinsic value, distributions, financing, exercise style and actual holder decisions matter. Writers can face assignment despite low perceived probability, and the absence of recent assignments is not a guarantee [M3]. The checker should reject categorical probability claims lacking support without inventing a replacement probability.

Delta describes local option-price sensitivity under a model/convention. It can be used as a rough market-model proxy for finishing in the money in some contexts, but it is not a universal empirical forecast and is not the probability of early assignment [M4]. A forward-looking estimate can be legitimate when its meaning and assumptions are explicit. The correct response to an unlabeled “certain” claim is a calibrated qualification, not an equally unsupported statement that assignment cannot happen.

An ITM option with substantial observed time value can make an assertion of inevitable immediate exercise poorly supported; it does not make early exercise impossible. Intrinsic value comparisons require matching underlying price and option quote timing, applicable units and terms. Dividends can affect prices and exercise incentives, but an ex-date does not guarantee a total-return outcome or an exact contemporaneous share move. Avoid declaring an exchange or broker rule from general intuition; the supplied applicable contract/policy governs.

## Rolls and action comparisons

For a specific proposed short-option roll, indicative net opening credit is new-option bid proceeds minus old-option ask buyback cost, with matched quantities, multipliers and observation conditions. Costs and execution can change it. Lower strike or later expiry alone determines neither debit nor credit. If exact replacement quotes are missing, the net cash flow is unresolved rather than inherently positive or negative. A clearly conditional statement about the price at which a roll would be acceptable is not a falsely quoted trade.

Management alternatives contain both facts and preferences. “I prefer to reduce this exposure because the downside no longer fits the objective” may be a valid judgment. “This close recovers the original premium,” “the roll is free,” or “the hedge disappears at this expiry” can be tested against the actual quantities and cash flows. Correct the premise and let the author reconsider the judgment. Do not translate every factual defect into the same action.
