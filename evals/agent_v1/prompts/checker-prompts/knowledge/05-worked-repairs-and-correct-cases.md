# Worked repairs and valid counterexamples

All names, contracts, quantities and prices here are invented teaching inputs. None describes an account, observed security, owner preference or current opportunity. The cases explain transferable relationships, not fixed answers or a mandatory reasoning order. The author retains responsibility for integrating a correction and reconsidering its implications. A checker reports the defect; it does not write the investment decision.

A correct diagnosis can still contain a faulty repair. Supporting explanations, headings, tables, parenthetical remarks and proposed wording carry the same obligation to preserve signs, scope and evidentiary limits. A sound final sentence does not cancel an unsupported assertion earlier in the reply. Preserve the known consequence without adding an unnecessary Greek, probability, funding assurance or other quantitative claim whose inputs are missing.

## A. The sign changes the exposure

Supplied snapshot: an invented issuer's December put has quantity +2, multiplier 100 and signed aggregate basis +460; the broker convention explicitly says positive is long. Draft: “These two short puts can assign 200 shares to the account.” This is a material confirmed error. The sign establishes two long puts with exercise rights, not two writer obligations. A repair says “The snapshot records two owned puts; selling two identical contracts would close those longs under ordinary net-position treatment.” Whether to retain, sell or exercise remains the author's judgment. The same arithmetic would not prove that a later snapshot remained unchanged.

## B. Units and basis survive the correction

Synthetic standard short puts: quantity −3, strike 7, 100 shares per contract, signed aggregate book basis −480. Draft: “Assignment needs $21,000, and the shares cost $6.84 after premium.” Gross strike payment is 3 × 100 × $7 = $2,100. If −480 is the stated aggregate signed book basis, book-basis-adjusted acquisition is ($2,100 − $480)/300 = $5.40 per share. The correction labels the book convention; it does not claim verified full historical premium cash flow. Neither $2,100 nor $1,620 is established incremental margin. If the draft already uses those labels and numbers, there is no defect simply because full transaction history is absent.

## C. Width is a payoff bound

Two compatible same-expiry put spreads each hold strike 82 and short strike 75, multiplier 100. Their aggregate net book debit is $480. Draft: “Maximum expiration profit is $1,400.” The maximum intrinsic spread payoff is 2 × 100 × (82 − 75) = $1,400; maximum book-based expiration profit is $920 before costs. Feedback identifies both quantities and the compatibility assumptions. A useful repaired draft can still favor the spread, reject it, or compare closing it. A spread with a different expiry on one leg would need a different analysis, not merely a different debit in this formula.

## D. A similar strike is a different contract

The original synthetic rows are: June 90 put +1 at per-unit mark 8.10, September 90 put +1 at 9.30, September 80 put −1 at 4.20; multiplier 100 throughout. Draft pairs the September short with the June mark, then adds both long puts as standalone assets. The September pair's signed mark is ($9.30 − $4.20) × 100 = $510. The June long's mark is $810. Additive total is $1,320, with every row used once. Other analytical groupings are possible, but a cross-expiry pair is not the same September vertical. The error is identity/allocation, not the mere use of a different grouping label.

## E. Closing side and date

A dated public snapshot for a matching synthetic contract shows bid 1.10 and ask 1.45. Three standard long contracts have indicative gross sale proceeds of 3 × 100 × 1.10 = $330. Three shorts have indicative gross buyback cost of 3 × 100 × 1.45 = $435. Draft: “The shorts can be bought back for $330 today.” This reverses side and overstates freshness/execution. The repair can cite the dated ask-side estimate, before fees and fill uncertainty. “I would seek a 1.25 limit, if the market supports it” is a conditional proposal, not a false claim that $375 was executable or filled.

## F. Entry basis is not an earlier market mark

At snapshot T0, the source gives a short's per-unit entry basis 2.70 and mark 9.60. At T1 a public quote has ask 9.10. Draft: “The option rose from 2.70 at T0 to 9.10, producing a new margin shock.” The first operand is entry basis, not T0 market value. The supplied T0 mark and T1 ask also use different measurement bases. Neither establishes the alleged market jump or broker margin change. A repair preserves the entry and valuation facts with their meanings and leaves a margin-change claim unsupported unless actual comparable account evidence exists. The checker must not invent a margin improvement from the lower later ask either.

## G. Obligation is not margin consumption

A synthetic account snapshot reports excess liquidity of $18,000. Standard short-put gross assignment funding under an all-assigned scenario is $12,600. Draft: “The remaining margin cushion would be $5,400.” Subtraction is arithmetically correct but the conclusion uses incompatible quantities. The repair separates the gross strike scenario from the broker's reported maintenance cushion and identifies the missing projected post-event requirements/assets. It does not declare the account insolvent, assume a cash-only mandate or recommend liquidation. A draft that already says “These figures alone cannot establish post-assignment capacity” is valid on that point.

The same distinction works in both directions: enough identified cash to pay this purchase does not settle the rest of the account's margin requirements, while a purchase exceeding identified cash does not establish a breach without the relevant borrowing and account evidence. “Funding on these stated resources is short; broker compliance is unresolved” can be correct. “Additional funds would cover this cash gap” answers that funding question only, even when phrased conditionally.

## H. The snippet exists, the full filing does not

The retained search excerpt quotes an invented company's revenue as $48 million, identifies a quarterly release, and is marked primary-body-not-inspected. Draft: “We inspected the filing and confirmed $48 million in audited annual revenue.” The material confirmed errors are the inspection claim and the change from quarterly excerpt to audited annual result. The underlying $48 million need not be fabricated. A repair says what the retained excerpt actually reports and leaves primary-document/period qualification visible. If its decisive investment conclusion needs an inspected annual figure, the author must obtain that evidence through authorized research or retain the dependency. “The excerpt reports $48 million; annual scope is not established” needs no repeated false alarm.

## I. Current moneyness does not settle a future event

A synthetic American-style put is currently in the money. Its matching quoted value includes material extrinsic value, and expiry is months away. Draft: “It will almost certainly be assigned tomorrow because it is in the money.” The record does not establish that probability or timing. Feedback asks for calibrated wording: early assignment is possible; present moneyness does not prove imminent assignment or the expiry outcome. “Assignment is a risk worth planning for” is not a confirmed error. A delta of −0.62, if also supplied, would not by itself supply a 62% early-assignment probability.

## J. A roll price cannot be deduced from its labels

Draft: “Moving the short put to a lower strike and later expiry must cost a debit.” The current short has a dated ask, but no replacement bid is supplied. Debit/credit depends on both exact legs, quantities, multipliers, costs and execution. The conclusion is unsupported rather than numerically disproven. An acceptable repair is “The net cash flow is not established; at a replacement bid above the matched close cost the illustrated roll would be a credit before fees.” This conditional relation neither creates a quote nor makes the roll attractive.

## K. Source identity and owner attribution

A public excerpt establishes only the invented issuer's name and quoted price. Draft assigns it a specific industry and says the owner holds it as an inflation hedge. The owner's actual supplied statement calls it a speculative experiment without that rationale. Public evidence does not establish the industry; the attribution of an owner thesis is unsupported. The correction narrows both statements to what their separate sources establish. It does not replace the invented thesis with another. A public Analyst must not receive the owner statement to investigate the issuer; the host/private Expert handles that attribution.

## L. A faithful internal summary can be short

An Expert submits a draft whose analysis says: “If a fresh matched bid confirms acceptable proceeds, I prefer reducing the position; the current retained quote is dated. Otherwise holding remains an alternative, with the specified risk.” Its internal summary says: “Sell immediately; the proceeds are confirmed.” The summary drops the condition, upgrades the evidence and removes the alternative's meaning. That is material inconsistency within the submitted draft. “I favor a conditional reduction, pending a fresh quote, with holding as an alternative” is a faithful shorter summary. The checker need not demand every analysis paragraph in the summary. This example creates no separate Lead, Explorer or Hermes check requirement.

## M. A bounded hypothesis is valid

Analyst draft: “This announced capacity project could raise demand for some suppliers; issuer-specific exposure and commercial terms need investigation.” The selected announcement supports what was announced, not beneficiary earnings or guaranteed project completion. This can be a valid research hypothesis. It should not be marked false merely because a full valuation model is absent. If the draft says named suppliers have already earned a specified incremental profit, that new assertion needs evidence. Checking must preserve room for exploration.

## N. Missing information need not invalidate everything

An Analyst accurately reports inspected annual revenue and explicitly says the option quote request failed, so no executable options comparison is offered. The unavailable quote does not invalidate the supported revenue observation. A private Expert may accurately calculate dated book P&L while saying current margin capacity is unknown. These do not establish support for claims elsewhere: an executive summary that then says capacity is ample would reintroduce a material unsupported claim.

## O. Resolve the finding rather than remember an answer

Initial draft calls a $700 gross payoff “profit” while the submitted premises include a $210 debit. The checker flags the $490 net result. The author explicitly calls again with the revised full draft and relevant premises; the fresh checker has no prior dialogue. The revised draft accurately discusses gross payoff, a separate debit and $490 profit. That reasoning is supported under those premises. If its summary still says $700 profit, the defect remains material. If the author supplies a different supported debit, the checker uses that current submission rather than an old answer. Neither this example's $490 nor earlier favorable feedback is an answer for other contracts, and a hypothetical debit is not a verified actual purchase.
