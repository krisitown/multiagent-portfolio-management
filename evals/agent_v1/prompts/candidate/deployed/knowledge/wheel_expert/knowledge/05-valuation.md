# 05 — Valuation, required returns and ownership economics

DRAFT knowledge. Valuation is a conditional estimate, not a tradable quote or a protective barrier. The objective is to understand what expectations a price requires and how much uncertainty the investor is accepting.

## Valuation objects and model consistency

Distinguish operating enterprise value from equity value and per-share value. A cash-flow model for all capital providers differs from a model for common shareholders. CFA's free-cash-flow summary pairs FCFF with WACC and FCFE with the required return on equity. A bridge from operating value to common equity may need debt, excess cash, nonoperating assets, preferred claims, minority interests and other adjustments. The exact bridge depends on what the starting value includes. Avoid counting cash or subtracting debt twice. [S17](../sources.md#s17)

For an illustrative nonfinancial company, a simplified operating expression is:

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + depreciation and amortization − capital expenditure − increase in operating working capital.

This is a model definition under stated assumptions, not a command to copy a reported subtotal. Taxes, leases, noncash charges and financial institutions can require different treatment. A common relationship is FCFE = FCFF − after-tax interest + net borrowing. Discounting FCFE with WACC or deducting debt again after an equity valuation mixes frameworks.

Projected nominal cash flows and discount rates must use consistent currencies and inflation assumptions. A real cash-flow forecast requires a real discount rate. If currency conversion is needed, state whether it occurs in forecast cash flows or valuation outputs and why the assumptions are coherent. A mechanically precise result built from mismatched units is invalid.

## Growth, reinvestment and incremental returns

Growth generally requires an economic explanation: units, prices, market share, product mix, acquisitions or some combination. It also requires resources. In a steady analytical relationship, operating growth can be linked to reinvestment rate and return on incremental capital. Damodaran's growth materials emphasize that current average returns need not equal returns on new investment. [S18](../sources.md#s18)

Therefore, a forecast that gives a company rapid perpetual growth, unchanged high margins and negligible reinvestment deserves scrutiny. An asset-light model can still require development, selling effort or acquisitions, even when accounting capital expenditure is modest. The question is not whether growth is desirable in the abstract, but whether the incremental investment earns enough to justify its cost and risk.

Avoid interpreting a high historical return on invested capital mechanically. Book capital can be affected by writeoffs, acquisitions, intangibles and accounting policy. Low measured capital can inflate a ratio. Explain the denominator and whether the estimate tells us anything about future investments.

## Terminal value and sensitivity

For a constant-growth cash-flow stream, terminal value at the forecast endpoint is next-period cash flow divided by the relevant discount rate minus the perpetual growth rate. The discount rate must exceed growth in this model. The terminal value must then be discounted back to the valuation date. A small difference between discount rate and growth can create a very large estimate; that is sensitivity, not evidence of a valuable company. [S17](../sources.md#s17)

A mature terminal state should be economically plausible. Market size, competition, reinvestment, margins and financing should be consistent with it. A terminal multiple is not free of assumptions; it embeds them in a market comparison. Do not combine an optimistic terminal multiple with an optimistic forecast and describe the apparent precision as conservatism.

Use sensitivity to reveal dependence. It can be more useful to say that the ownership case fails if a margin stays depressed than to display dozens of scarcely distinguishable valuations. Scenarios can represent coherent combinations, such as lower sales, weaker margins and tighter funding. Varying one input at a time is informative but may miss these relationships.

## Relative valuation and reverse reasoning

A P/E or enterprise-value multiple is a price relative to a specified accounting or forecast measure. Compare like definitions, periods and claims. Enterprise value to an equity-only earnings measure can mismatch numerator and denominator. Loss-making companies do not become cheap because a P/E is negative. Peer medians can be useful context, but an entire peer group can be expensively priced. CFA's market-based valuation materials describe both the usefulness and limitations of multiples. [S19](../sources.md#s19)

Reverse valuation asks what future performance would be needed to support the observed price under declared assumptions. It can clarify disagreement without claiming to know the correct growth rate. A stock may require only modest growth but still be risky because its balance sheet is fragile. Another may require sustained growth that is possible but already fully reflected in price.

A margin of safety is a judgment about price relative to uncertainty, not an automatic percentage. A large discount to a poorly supported estimate is weak evidence. A smaller discount to resilient, transparent cash flows may or may not be adequate for the owner. Do not invent their required discount.

## Translating valuation into option research

Effective entry from a short put reflects strike less net premium under an explicit economic convention. It is not a tax-basis determination and does not guarantee that shares will be worth that amount when assigned. Compare entry economics with plausible ownership values, not only current spot. The investor also gives up flexibility by accepting an obligation while waiting.

Evaluate the opportunity cost of nonassignment. If the owner wants exposure to a strongly appreciating business, repeatedly selling puts may leave them with premiums but no shares. If the owner mainly wants patient, price-sensitive entry, that same outcome may be acceptable. Neither preference should be inferred.

A covered call makes a different bargain: retain some downside exposure while exchanging some upside for premium over the contract period. Compare the total position with holding shares alone, closing some exposure or waiting. Original purchase price is relevant history and may matter for tax, but it is not a valuation floor or a universal strike-selection rule.

The resulting recommendation can say that the company is high quality, the stock is approximately fairly valued, but the available option does not compensate for illiquidity or concentration. Keeping these conclusions separate makes the opinion more useful than a single score that hides the reason.
