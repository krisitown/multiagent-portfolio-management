# 12 — Portfolio construction, concentration and correlated obligations

DRAFT knowledge. A trade can be reasonable on its own and inappropriate in a portfolio. Assess interactions using supplied positions and clearly defined scenarios; do not infer a complete household balance sheet from a brokerage snapshot.

## Multiple views of exposure

Position market value, contractual notional, delta-equivalent exposure, assignment funding and stressed loss measure different things. A short option's small current market value can coexist with a large contingent share purchase. A share holding and short puts on the same issuer can increase ownership concentration together. If a summary presents one exposure number, its definition must be clear.

Issuer and sector concentration are useful starting views, but common economic drivers can cross sector labels. Rates, consumer spending, commodity prices, a concentrated customer base, funding conditions or a common supply chain can connect apparently diverse companies. Conversely, businesses sharing a sector label can have distinct economics. Qualitative factor discussion is appropriate when robust quantitative estimates are unavailable.

Diversification can reduce exposure to company-specific outcomes, but it does not remove all market risk. The effect depends on weights, volatility and co-movement, not simply the count of tickers. CFA's portfolio-risk reading treats these relationships, while SEC education relates allocation to horizon and risk tolerance. [S25](../sources.md#s25) [S28](../sources.md#s28)

## Correlation and nonlinear exposure

For two simple return series, portfolio variance includes weighted individual variances and a cross term involving correlation. That algebra helps explain why two assets can diversify one another. Historical estimates depend on sample, frequency, currency and changing conditions. They should not be extrapolated into guaranteed protection during a crisis.

Options make the picture more complicated because sensitivities change with price, time and volatility. A portfolio of short puts may appear modestly exposed at one moment and become more equity-sensitive after a decline. A linear delta summary can help describe a local condition, but stress scenarios should consider the changing payoff and financing consequences.

A single correlation estimate can hide dependence in severe outcomes. Two companies may both face refinancing stress even if their historical weekly prices did not move closely together. The appropriate response is not to invent a higher correlation; it is to disclose the limitation and examine a relevant joint scenario.

## Assignment and expiry clustering

Map the periods when obligations coexist, not only their expiry dates. A new later-expiry option opened while an earlier option remains open creates simultaneous exposure until the earlier position is closed, expires or changes through assignment. Assignment can replace an option with shares and cash obligations, so it need not end the underlying exposure. A proposal to wait until an earlier exit is conditional on that exit; it is not the same as opening now. Spreading expirations changes timing but does not itself prevent stacking, remove common underlying risk or eliminate early assignment. Several obligations can draw on the same funding or face the same event even with different expiries.

If the discovery list has five candidates, treat them initially as alternatives. A discussion of taking several requires a combined analysis, including existing holdings. Do not add five separate one-contract “affordable” conclusions and infer that the basket is affordable. The same cash, eligible shares or hedge cannot support multiple incompatible claims.

Coverage matching must respect quantity and terms. Shared long options, covered shares, overlapping strategies and pending assignments can produce double counting. A summary can preserve both raw holdings and a matched-exposure view, with unmatched amounts explicit. Unsupported assets stay visible even if their risk cannot be modeled.

## Sizing as a constrained judgment

Sizing depends on the owner's objectives, losses they can tolerate, financing, liquidity, diversification and desired management burden. A premium target is not a size limit. A trader's willingness to own one lot does not imply willingness to own many lots simultaneously after a market decline.

Risk willingness, risk capacity and required return are distinct. Willingness concerns preferences; capacity concerns financial ability to absorb losses and meet obligations; required return concerns goals and resources. They can conflict. Without sufficient information, the expert should not compress them into a fictional risk score.

Small positions can still be operationally complex, while concentrated positions can require little routine activity until something changes. Management burden is therefore not proportional only to notional size. Event calendars, number of distinct issuers, expiries, currencies and dependence on active intervention matter.

## Performance and attribution

NAV change is not automatically investment return. Deposits, withdrawals, transfers, currency and price changes can all affect account value. Time-weighted and money-weighted measures answer different questions and require appropriate history. If that history is absent, describe observable value changes rather than inventing performance.

A wheel report should not count option premiums as income while hiding share losses elsewhere. Attribute results consistently across cash, options, shares, dividends and financing. The owner can care about cash distributions, but cash flow and total return must remain separately visible. A preference for regular receipts does not make principal losses economically disappear.

Portfolio conclusions should state the snapshot used and its gaps. A new download of old positions remains old evidence. If discovery and management use incompatible versions, reconcile them or qualify the affected conclusions; do not call the combined report current merely because it was assembled today.
