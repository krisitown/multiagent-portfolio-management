# 02 — Financial statements, cash flow and quality of information

DRAFT knowledge. The purpose of accounting analysis is to understand the business's economics and financing, not to award points for a familiar ratio. Public filings are primary evidence about what the issuer reports; they are not independent confirmation that every estimate or forecast is correct.

## What the statements describe

A balance sheet is a snapshot of assets, liabilities and equity at a date. Income and cash-flow statements cover a period; equity statements help reconcile changes in owners' claims. Mixing a quarter's profit with a year's revenue or using mismatched balance-sheet dates can make a plausible-looking ratio meaningless. Annual and interim filings differ in coverage and audit status. SEC's EDGAR guidance describes 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K and proxy filings and why each can matter to research. Foreign issuers may use different forms. [S10](../sources.md#s10)

Choose periods deliberately. Trailing-twelve-month figures may be built from a latest annual period plus subsequent interim results less the comparable prior interim period, but only with consistent definitions. Fiscal calendars, acquisitions, divestitures, restatements and currency changes complicate the bridge. A vendor's TTM label is insufficient if the result is decisive. Ask for the underlying calculation or retain uncertainty.

Revenue growth can reflect higher unit sales, price increases, currency translation, acquisitions or changes in recognition. These are economically different. An acquisition can add reported revenue while increasing debt and dilution. A price increase can improve margin while weakening future demand. A useful explanation relates the numbers to operations rather than simply repeating the growth rate.

## Cash generation and reinvestment

Net income is not cash available to shareholders. Depreciation, amortization, receivables, inventories, payables and other accruals affect reconciliation. Capital expenditure consumes cash outside an ordinary operating-cash-flow subtotal. Debt proceeds can increase cash without representing operating success. A dividend or buyback can be financed by borrowing even while headline distributions appear stable.

A common descriptive free-cash-flow measure is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure. It is a definition, not a universally standardized valuation input. It may differ from free cash flow to the firm or equity because of interest classification, financing flows and other adjustments. Keep reported measures, analyst adjustments and valuation cash flows separately named. Do not silently switch definitions across peers or years. CFA's financial-analysis materials emphasize comparability, industry context and interpretation of ratios rather than isolated computation. [S11](../sources.md#s11)

Consider the cause and persistence of cash changes. Reducing inventory can release cash once while reflecting lower demand or improved efficiency. Extending supplier payment terms can improve operating cash flow without improving the underlying margin. Rising deferred revenue may be economically favorable but carries future service obligations. The same accounting pattern can support competing hypotheses; a label alone does not establish manipulation or strength.

Stock-based compensation illustrates the distinction between current cash and ownership. A noncash charge can be added back in cash-flow presentation while still transferring value through dilution. For analysis, reconcile share count, grants, repurchases and forecast per-share outcomes. Avoid automatically treating every buyback as value creation: the purchase price, financing and offsetting issuance matter.

## Report quality versus business results

Accurate reporting can faithfully describe a weak business. Conversely, apparently strong earnings may be distorted by aggressive recognition or nonrecurring gains. CFA distinguishes reporting quality from the economic quality and persistence of results. This distinction prevents the analyst from equating a cleanly presented statement with a durable ownership thesis. [S12](../sources.md#s12)

Potential investigation areas include changing accounting policies, recurring exclusions from adjusted earnings, receivables growing unusually fast relative to sales, acquisitions obscuring organic performance, unusual capitalization of expenses, related-party dealings, large contingent obligations and material weaknesses disclosed by the issuer. These are prompts for evidence, not accusations of fraud. A financial institution's balance sheet or an early-stage business may legitimately look different from an industrial benchmark.

Read management's explanation as a claim to be tested. Reconcile adjusted earnings to the applicable reported measure, identify what has been excluded, and ask whether similar charges recur. A cost labeled restructuring for several years may be economically recurring even if individually unusual. Do not add back losses simply to make the company pass a quality test.

## Ratios as relationships

A ratio has a numerator, denominator, units and period. Interest coverage based on EBIT differs from EBITDA coverage; gross debt differs from net debt; average equity differs from ending equity. A very high return on equity may reflect substantial leverage or a small equity base after repurchases. Negative equity can make customary ratios misleading. An apparently low P/E based on peak cyclical earnings can conceal expensive normalized economics.

Useful comparisons may be with the company's own history, competitors with similar economics, management's past claims and conditions under stress. Peer similarity must be justified: accounting choices, business mix, geography and capital intensity can dominate sector labels. If a ratio cannot be interpreted with the available information, report its calculation without pretending it establishes quality.

For wheel analysis, the key consequence is the credibility of ownership after an adverse move. Explain how cash generation and obligations would behave in the scenario that triggers assignment. Strong recent cash flow matters less if it arose from a temporary windfall while refinancing and capital needs are approaching. Equally, a temporary cash shortfall need not invalidate a durable company if financing and future economics are well supported. Judgment depends on the full evidence, not one desirable sign.
