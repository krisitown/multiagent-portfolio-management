# 14 — Behavioral finance, incentives and decision quality

DRAFT knowledge. Behavioral concepts help inspect reasoning. They are not diagnoses of the owner or claims that awareness eliminates bias.

## Reference points and loss framing

Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory describes how choices under risk can depend on reference points and how gains and losses are evaluated. It is a theory informed by experiments, not a validated recipe for managing a particular short put. Applying it here means noticing when a decision is framed entirely around recovering a previous price or avoiding the label of a realized loss. [S27](../sources.md#s27)

The economically relevant alternatives start from present opportunities and obligations, but transaction history can still matter for taxes, constraints and learning. A useful question is whether the proposed replacement would be attractive if the old loss were displayed separately. This is an analytical prompt, not a requirement to disregard history.

Mental accounting can make premium receipts feel like income while losses on assigned shares remain psychologically separate. The report can preserve a cash-flow view for budgeting and a total-economic-result view for assessment. Showing both helps avoid an argument over labels while making consequences visible.

## Belief errors and information selection

CFA's behavioral-bias reading distinguishes several cognitive and emotional patterns, including overconfidence, anchoring and confirmation-related errors. These categories can guide review without establishing that any particular person exhibits them. [S26](../sources.md#s26)

A research team can reproduce the same error in parallel if all members begin from one bullish narrative. Independent source collection is useful only when alternative explanations are permitted. The expert can request the strongest supported countercase, ask which fact would reverse a conclusion, or identify where an analyst's claim outruns the evidence. These are available methods rather than a compulsory adversarial ritual.

Hindsight can make an uncertain event look predictable after it happens. Outcome bias can reward a weak decision that happened to profit or condemn a sensible decision that lost. Evaluate what was knowable at the decision time, whether the proposed exposure fit the constraints, and whether uncertainty was represented honestly.

## Incentives and convenient measures

A candidate quota can bias research toward filling slots. A premium target can favor volatile or concentrated exposures. A desire to appear active can produce unnecessary adjustments. A desire to avoid error can produce endless requests for more data. These are risks of process design, not evidence that a specific analyst has acted improperly.

Use metrics as descriptions rather than substitutes for purpose. Win rate, collected premium, number of ideas, annualized yield and time spent researching can each improve while investment usefulness deteriorates. The relevant judgment combines downside, capital use, evidence quality, alternatives and management effort.

## Decisions under irreducible uncertainty

More research is valuable when it can plausibly change a consequential decision. It is less valuable when it only adds detail around an unchanged conclusion. Identify the unresolved assumption and the consequence of being wrong. The lead can then decide whether to commission work within budget.

A reversible decision to wait may preserve flexibility, but waiting is not costless when it misses exposure or leaves existing risk unresolved. An irreversible or difficult-to-reverse commitment may justify stronger evidence. The degree of scrutiny should relate to consequences, not the agent's enthusiasm.

Communicate uncertainty specifically. “The business appears strong, but current broker capacity is missing” is more useful than a generic disclaimer. “Two sources disagree on the dividend date” is more actionable than a vague low-confidence score. A clear conditional opinion often serves the owner better than false certainty or complete refusal to judge.
