# 06 — Option identity, rights and contract boundaries

DRAFT knowledge. Product-specific terms outrank generic shorthand. This library concentrates on listed US equity options; it does not silently transfer their mechanics to indexes, futures, foreign options or unsupported instruments.

## What identifies the obligation

An option record needs a stable underlying/security identity, put or call, strike, expiry, currency, quantity, multiplier and deliverable/adjustment details. Venue or provider identifiers can help reconcile sources but must not replace the economic terms. A ticker alone is insufficient for matching hedges or coverage. Moneyness compares the same contract's strike with a compatible underlying price: a put is in the money below strike and a call above strike, independent of whether held long or short. An unsupported underlying price leaves current moneyness unresolved; a hypothetical price supports only a conditional statement. Similar-looking expiries or adjusted symbols can represent different obligations.

OCC describes standard equity options as covering 100 shares, with American-style exercise and physical share delivery. Corporate actions can produce adjusted contracts representing something else. Its specification states that exercise notices normally produce stock delivery on the following business day, T+1. These are current product mechanics as checked on the source-access date, not a reason to skip contract-specific verification. [S01](../sources.md#s01)

For a standard contract, a premium quoted at 2.00 ordinarily corresponds to 200 in contract currency when the multiplier is 100. Under a positive-long/negative-short source convention, long contracts sum positive quantities and short contracts sum absolute negative quantities; gross contracts are their sum, while signed net is their difference within a stated scope. Position rows, distinct series and tax lots are separate counts. Reconcile overlapping summaries and lots rather than counting both. A signed net across strikes, rights or expiries is not residual risk or hedge coverage. Counts by right or underlying must retain the same scope as their totals; do not infer portfolio counts from narrative mentions. A long cannot reduce gross short inventory, though compatible protection may reduce a separately defined exposure measure.

An adjusted contract may deliver shares, cash or a combination. A simple strike-times-100 calculation can be wrong for its exercise consideration or deliverable interpretation. Use the applicable OCC information memo and a calculator capable of representing the terms. If that capability is absent, preserve the position as unmodeled rather than converting it into a standard contract by assumption. [S33](../sources.md#s33)

## Rights, obligations and coverage

For physically settled share options, the four stock transitions differ. Short-put assignment buys the deliverable and pays strike cash. Short-call assignment sells/delivers it and receives strike cash. Long-call exercise buys it and pays strike cash. Long-put exercise sells/delivers it and receives strike cash. Each transition applies only to contracts actually exercised/assigned; remaining contracts and existing stock persist. Starting with no shares, a permitted/effected put exercise or short-call assignment can create short stock; it never creates positive delivery shares. Broker permissions, borrow, funding and exercise instructions remain separate. Closing the option for cash is a different transaction and does not itself exercise it. Cash-settled products do not follow this share ledger. An anticipated exit is not a current hedge. [S02](../sources.md#s02) [S46](../sources.md#s46)

Covered-call analysis requires eligible, available deliverable shares in sufficient quantity without double counting shares committed to other short calls or obligations. A long put cannot be added as potential positive shares. A compatible long call can hedge a short call economically, but is not an existing stock holding: exercise needs strike cash, valid timing and the correct deliverable. Same-expiry matched quantities can bound terminal spread payoff without identical strikes. With compatible underlying, right, currency, multiplier and deliverable, a long call at or below the short-call strike protects the matched upside obligation; a long put at or above the short-put strike protects the matched downside obligation. Opposite strike ordering leaves a strike-gap loss before premiums and costs. This is payoff geometry, not evidence that the legs were opened together or that the whole trade is profitable. Excess shorts remain exposed and one long cannot protect multiple shorts at once. Unknown deliverables make exact protection unverified, not absent; describe the visible potential relationship conditionally without assuming standard terms. Different expiries, adjusted terms, early assignment and independent exercise can change the bound or funding path. [S50](../sources.md#s50) A correlated security, another share class or an ADR is not automatically the required deliverable. Distinguish stock coverage, option-payoff protection and broker recognition. [S47](../sources.md#s47)

Strategy names follow the signed legs. For equal quantities of compatible same-expiry calls, long lower strike/short higher strike is a bull-call spread; reversing those sides is a bear-call spread. For puts, long higher strike/short lower strike is a bear-put spread; reversing those sides is a bull-put spread. The bullish or bearish name describes direction, not a promise of profit. Entry cash and costs determine profit separately from terminal payoff. A credit received on the short leg alone does not make the combined spread a credit strategy. Missing spot prevents current moneyness, but not conditional payoff decomposition from known terms. An earlier option's expiry leaves a separate later-expiry long/short pair in place unless a stated exercise, assignment or transaction changes it. Missing deliverable evidence limits certification of that pair; it does not prove that the long disappears or that the remaining short is unbounded. [S50](../sources.md#s50)

Use covered precisely: actual eligible deliverable shares, conditional option-payoff protection and broker-recognized coverage are different claims. A covered call retains substantial share downside. A matched long does not cancel the short's gross assignment cash or guarantee coordinated exercise. [S04](../sources.md#s04) [S47](../sources.md#s47)

## Time and calendars

DTE needs a stated valuation date and convention. Calendar DTE is expiry date minus that date; using an older quote date produces a different interval. Do not mix either interval with trading-session counts or use a stale DTE in an annualization. Local dates and UTC offsets can change across daylight-saving transitions. The last trading time, contractual expiration, exercise-instruction deadline, assignment notice and settlement are distinct; neither a market close nor a date alone establishes a broker deadline.

A position around expiration can change from an option into shares and cash obligations before an updated snapshot. Present holdings and anticipated shares are different states; adding them can double count. Event exposure has both an entry and an exit boundary. An event preceding entry is outside that position's holding interval, although its consequences can affect entry conditions. For an option open before a 12 March event, 20 March expiry includes the event; 6 March expiry precedes it. Retained shares after assignment can still face 12 March risk. Same-date ordering needs session and timezone evidence; an estimated event remains estimated. A scenario does not establish that assignment or any other transaction occurred.

Corporate-action and identity uncertainty can be more urgent than price refinement. A calculation with a narrow bid/ask spread is still unusable if it describes the wrong deliverable. Exact terms are a necessary foundation for arithmetic; they do not require the agent to follow one particular research sequence.
