# 19 — Interest rates, inflation, currency and peripheral exposures

DRAFT knowledge. Macro context is useful when it changes a company's economics, valuation, funding or alternatives. It is not a requirement to forecast every economic release.

## Rates affect several parts of the decision

Rates can influence borrowing expense, discount rates, customer demand, refinancing and the return available on cash. The direction and magnitude depend on contract terms and the business. A rate increase can benefit one revenue stream while hurting another. Avoid a universal claim that a particular sector or option strategy always benefits from higher rates.

Fixed-rate bond prices generally move inversely to relevant yields, with the effect depending on maturity, coupon and other characteristics. Government credit backing does not remove market-price sensitivity before maturity. SEC's fixed-income bulletin explains this distinction. [S37](../sources.md#s37)

This matters if bonds are treated as collateral or a cash reserve. A long-duration security is not interchangeable with settlement cash simply because it is issued by a government. Even short-duration instruments have settlement, eligibility and price considerations. The team must verify what resources the broker recognizes rather than infer it from asset labels.

Credit spreads and risk-free rates are different components of financing conditions. A company can face rising borrowing costs even if policy rates fall, because its own credit risk or market liquidity changes. A refinancing assumption should identify the debt involved, timing and scenario rather than simply applying the latest policy rate.

## Inflation and business resilience

Inflation affects costs, customers and required returns in different ways. Pricing power is the ability to preserve economics under relevant conditions, not merely the ability to announce higher prices. Wage, input, financing and working-capital pressures can arrive at different times. A company may grow nominal revenue while unit demand or real purchasing power weakens.

Real and nominal measures should not be mixed. An expected nominal return does not describe purchasing-power growth without an inflation assumption. A long-term ownership thesis can be plausible while the investor's real return disappoints. Conversely, a company can pass through some cost increases without maintaining every historical margin.

Use macro data at the correct vintage. Releases can be revised and market expectations can change before the headline is published. The expert should not explain a live market move with a retrospective narrative presented as established causation. If the link is an inference, say so.

## Currency exposure

An instrument's trading currency, the company's operating currencies and the investor's reporting currency need not match. A US-listed issuer can have global revenues and costs; an ADR can introduce additional domicile and depositary considerations. A USD option obligation can require funding even when account equity is displayed in another currency.

CFA's currency-management material discusses the contribution of currency exposure to portfolio outcomes and the tradeoffs of hedging. Hedging can change risk while adding cost, implementation requirements and basis differences. There is no universal hedge ratio appropriate for every investor. [S32](../sources.md#s32)

Distinguish a company's internal hedges from the owner's currency exposure. Corporate hedging may stabilize an operating cash flow without fixing the reporting-currency value of the investor's shares. For a portfolio calculation, keep the translation rate and date explicit. Do not silently mix historical cost conversions with current exposure conversions.

## Broader alternatives and the mandate

Cash, bonds, diversified funds and other assets can be useful comparators for opportunity cost or portfolio resilience. They are not automatically in scope for new discovery recommendations. An alternative with a lower expected return may better match a near-term obligation, while another may require more liquidity or expertise than the owner has authorized.

The expert should understand enough peripheral finance to recognize these dependencies and request appropriate evidence. It need not create a complete macro forecast or financial plan before evaluating a company. Explain only the macro and peripheral factors that materially affect the current judgment.
