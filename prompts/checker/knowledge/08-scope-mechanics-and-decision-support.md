# Report failures: scope, mechanics and decision support

Fictional, anonymized examples distilled from observed report failures. All companies, amounts and dates below are synthetic. These are reasoning patterns, not account facts, policy limits or instructions to recommend a trade. Apply them only to evidence actually supplied. Preserve valid counterexamples and distinguish an unsupported claim from a demonstrated error.

## 1. A checked excerpt is not a checked full report
Submitted: a purported final-report check contains only six corrected figures. Faulty feedback: "The final report is verified; remaining changes are labels." You have not seen the position ledger, candidates, funding plan or final wording.
Required response: lead with "Scope: submitted claims only; the complete intended report was not supplied." Review those claims normally, name what is outside scope, and ask the author to supply the complete intended report if final-report validation is sought within its remaining cap. Do not claim to inspect unseen sections or grant extra calls. A later author must not expand this into whole-report approval.
Valid: a public analyst intentionally requests a narrow check of a valuation calculation; a scoped answer is sufficient and no full portfolio is required. The manager's full-report requirement does not apply to every narrow research question.

## 2. Moneyness comes from underlying price versus strike
Evidence: Northstar ETF spot $108 at time T, put strike $100, option mark $2.40. Faulty: "The put is 8% in the money because spot exceeds strike" or "its premium establishes moneyness." At T the put is out of the money: max(100 - 108, 0) = 0 intrinsic per share. The call at that strike is in the money. A later spot of $92 makes the put $8 in the money; preserve the observation date. If expressing a percentage, state whether the denominator is spot or strike.
Valid: "OTM at the supplied observation; still has downside/assignment exposure if prices change." Do not equate OTM with harmless or infer future exercise from current moneyness.

## 3. Check matched option structures before calling them uncovered
Evidence: +2 Meridian $80 calls, -2 $95 calls, same underlying, expiry, multiplier 100 and deliverable; net debit $900; separately owned 50 shares.
Faulty: "The 200-share short-call delivery exceeds 50 shares, so this is an unhedged naked call position." The matched lower-strike long calls are part of the risk analysis. Distinguish not share-covered from unhedged. At expiry the matched option package has a $3,000 maximum intrinsic payoff, $2,100 maximum profit and $900 maximum loss before costs under the stated terms. Gross delivery is not net economic risk or current short stock.
Valid: early assignment, expiration mismatches and broker procedures can create temporary stock/funding exposure; long calls do not make every operational risk disappear. If quantities, expiries or deliverables differ, identify the unmatched risk rather than assuming protection. Independently owned shares retain their own payoff; a vertical does not by itself cap their upside.

## 4. A gross cash gap is not proof of financing impossibility
Evidence: gross put settlement amount $40,000; cash/bond reserve $12,000; owner permits margin; current broker capacity absent.
Faulty: "The $28,000 gap proves assignment cannot be funded and requires selling the core," followed by a disclaimer that margin is unknown. The disclaimer does not repair the categorical conclusion.
Valid: "Under a cash-only assumption, reserve is short by $28,000. Margin-funded feasibility is unresolved; here are the inputs/risks that matter." A manager may recommend lower exposure, but label its reserve rule as a proposed policy and support the judgment rather than falsely attributing it to broker necessity or the owner. Conversely, do not infer that margin permission proves sufficient capacity. Keep gross delivery, cash settlement, borrowing and stressed liquidity separate.

## 5. Cash to close, P&L, basis, FX and return units
Evidence: one short put, 100 multiplier; current ask $4.50/share; opening credit verified $120; ignore fees. Buying it back spends $450 now and realizes a $330 loss. Faulty: "Closing costs $330," "the prior $120 is received again," or treating an unverified book basis as confirmed original trade proceeds. If only broker basis is supplied, qualify historical premium/P&L. If only a mark is supplied, label the result a mark-based estimate rather than an executable ask.
Evidence: asset value USD 1,200, EUR/USD conversion 0.90 EUR per USD. Base value is EUR 1,080; weight uses EUR NAV. Verify the supplied currency before converting; never convert an already-EUR owner planning figure again.
Evidence: $2.50 quoted per share means $250 for one standard 100-share contract, not $2.50 per contract. A proposed 60-day trade earns $2.50 on a $100 strike: simple gross-strike return is 2.5%, annualized about 15.21% using 365/60. Historical premium collected months ago cannot be divided by days remaining and described as the original holding-period return. Annualized scaling is not a forecast or monthly income.
Valid: separately labeled prospective return based on current premium and remaining time, or maximum payoff/debit ratio without claiming expected return.

## 6. Use the payoff-specific decision, not a generic ownership test
Faulty: "Close the protective long puts unless you would own the shares at their strikes." A long put gives a sale right, not the short put's contingent purchase obligation; willingness to buy is not its direct payoff test. Likewise, a short call in a matched call spread does not imply buying shares at that short strike.
Valid: judge a hedge by intended protection, cost, overlap, expiry and alternatives; judge a short put by purchase exposure and funding; judge a spread as matched legs plus any residual. Do not choose the owner's strategy for them or assume a hedge is useful merely because it is called insurance.
Faulty: "An ex-dividend date within the contract's life proves this put is expensive." A calendar event alone does not establish mispricing, expected return or exercise incentive. Require the actual option terms, valuation premise and dated evidence. Do not transpose a call-holder dividend exercise explanation onto puts.

## 7. Preserve disagreements and inspect dependent conclusions
Evidence: specialist headline says "roll Juniper"; its action section says "close all, including Juniper." Faulty manager claim: "The specialist unambiguously recommends closing Juniper." Flag internal inconsistency; the manager can choose a supported course but must not invent consensus or conceal the opposing passage.
Evidence: at unchanged NAV and price, integer sizing yields 39.7% versus a 40% target. Faulty correction: "39.7% is above 40%." Recheck the checker correction's inequality as well as its arithmetic. Below-target rounding may be acceptable if acknowledged; do not manufacture a material defect.
Correcting a table is insufficient if its action still relies on the old value, currency, moneyness or policy. Name both passages. Retain truthful conclusions and do not demand stylistic rewording as a financial repair.

## 8. Headings do not establish substantive coverage
For a supplied complete actionable report, "Every holding" is not fulfilled by omitting a material fund or merging different option strikes without identifying them. "New candidates" as unexplained ticker names does not establish researched fit; "contribution plan" without a funding choice or decision rule does not establish a funded implementation.
Flag a concrete false completeness claim or missing action-driving dependency when the packet establishes it. Do not require invented quotes, mandatory trades or arbitrary allocations to fill sections. A reasoned empty shortlist, explicit unknown, conditional plan or supported hold can be valid. Suggest preserving useful coverage during correction, not replacing the report with your findings. Do not impose portfolio-wide sections on scoped analyst work.
