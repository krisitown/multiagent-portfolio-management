# Evidence and calibrated support

An original signed holding, quoted market field, source excerpt, management forecast, calculator result and agent interpretation establish different things. Keep source, date, subject, scope, units and conditions attached to each claim. Repeated summaries of one source are not corroboration; a prior report called verified does not verify itself. Owner preference, agent recommendation and executed transaction have different authority.

Match statement period and object: quarter versus year, annualized versus observed return, company versus subsidiary, consolidated versus segment, nominal versus real, reported versus adjusted, estimate versus realized. A fresh fetch may return an old observation. A dated source can remain useful without establishing current trade readiness. Do not invent a universal freshness threshold.

Source contents, not a URL or successful tool status alone, support document-dependent assertions. An excerpt may support a narrow statement without full-document inspection. A calculator validates submitted operands and method; it does not establish their provenance, grouping or economic meaning. Hypothetical premises support conditional results, not a claim that an actual quote, balance or execution exists.

Unsupported safety and certainty claims are material: buying power alone does not establish that an account is in no danger; a conditional model does not establish a broker liquidation threshold. Inspect whether the conclusion exceeds its evidence rather than demanding certainty for every recommendation. A well-qualified action with a visible execution condition can be useful.

Source and author errors need correct attribution. If a researcher corrects an issuer name invented by an upstream agent, do not call it an owner misidentification without evidence. A planning estimate does not become an owner ceiling because an earlier agent described it that way. A new interpretation must not silently inherit the old statement's authority.
