# Inventory, units and financial arithmetic

Position sign determines long versus short; preserve actual quantity and instrument. Options require underlying, right, exact expiry, strike, multiplier, deliverable, exercise/settlement terms and currency. Standard contracts often represent 100 shares but adjusted and other products differ. A similar strike or expiry is a different contract. A source identity conflict must remain unresolved unless supplied originals settle it.

Price per share, cash per contract and position total are different scales. Apply quantity and multiplier once. A negative option holding is not a negative price. Book basis, current mark, closing cash, premium, payoff and profit are different quantities. A reported position total cannot be remultiplied by all contracts. Missing historical premiums may limit lifetime P&L while leaving a supported incremental close-cost comparison possible.

For long positions, closing sales ordinarily use bids; closing shorts ordinarily use asks. Fees and financing need consistent treatment. Hypothetical limits or midpoints must be labeled. A zero/zero market or an old unrelated quote cannot support a claim of a currently quoted two-sided opportunity.

Return = specified net gain or premium / specified capital. State horizon and whether the numerator is premium, realized profit, modeled payoff or total return. Simple annualized return = period return × 365 / elapsed days; a 30-day equivalent uses 30 / elapsed days. These scalings assume comparability and do not establish recurring income. Compounded annualization uses (1 + period return)^(365/days) − 1 under stated reinvestment assumptions. Return on margin is not return on strike funding or NAV. A percentage label must agree with its denominator and period everywhere in the draft.

Currency conversion uses the stated direction. At 1.10 USD/EUR, USD 55,000 / 1.10 = EUR 50,000. Currency balance and translated display value describe the same money. Compare matched dates or disclose mixed observations. Nominal account growth with contributions is not pure investment performance.

Allocation difference at unchanged NAV is (current weight − proposed target weight) × NAV, not target weight × NAV. Percentage points and percent changes differ. A rate rising from 4% to 5% increases one percentage point or 25% relative to its former value. Rounding cannot explain a materially different denominator or a missing position bucket.
