# Plans, flows and comparable alternatives

An owner's objective, soft preference, explicit limit, planning estimate and agent proposal have different authority. A planning estimate need not be fully spent, capped at that amount or treated as a required minimum. A recommendation that changes its meaning needs justification and attribution, not a silent rewrite in continuity.

Expected monthly contributions have an amount and arrival date. They are not currently settled cash. Proposed DCA purchases must be feasible under the stated flow or disclose another funding source. Future sale proceeds remain contingent; a contribution-only plan and a sale-proceeds plan are different scenarios. A fixed strategic allocation target and this month's practical purchases can differ without contradiction if explained.

Do not use the same cash, shares, hedge or borrowing capacity twice across supposedly simultaneous proposals. Mutually exclusive alternatives can each be reasonable; adding all their benefits is not a funded plan. A currency translation of cash is not another pool. A recommendation to borrow must reflect the resulting liability and costs rather than being called self-funded.

For each comparison preserve the same accounting horizon or explain differences. Entry debit/credit, realized profit, remaining risk, stock ownership after assignment and opportunity cost are different components. A long-term thesis can remain valid while a near-term option expires before its realization. A good public opportunity does not by itself establish private sizing. A supported conditional path is useful; no invented policy, execution claim or certainty is required.
