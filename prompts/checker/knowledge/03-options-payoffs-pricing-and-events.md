# Options, exposure and path

At expiry, long call payoff is max(S−K,0) and long put payoff max(K−S,0) per deliverable unit. Short positions reverse payoff; entry cash flow, quantity, multiplier and costs produce profit. A short put's maximum ordinary option profit is net premium; its loss if stock reaches zero is strike funding less that premium. A covered call includes stock downside and gives up some upside. A long option's premium-only loss bound applies to that position under stated disposition assumptions, not automatically to stock created by unmanaged exercise.

For an ordinary compatible same-expiry one-to-one vertical: debit-spread maximum expiry loss is debit and maximum profit width less debit; credit-spread maximum profit is credit and maximum loss width less credit. Scale by quantity/multiplier and include costs. Width alone is not loss. Verify signs and legs before trusting a strategy label. Excess short legs can introduce losses not bounded by a small entry debit or the nearest wing.

Terminal payoff, pre-expiry value, realized P&L, settlement cash and broker recognition are separate. A future option value depends on time, volatility, rates, distributions, exercise terms and the underlying path; today's quote cannot be assumed unchanged after a large move. For an ordinary put, intrinsic exposure under a hypothetical low stock price can directly contradict a tiny claimed close loss. Account for applicable style/discount assumptions before making an exact bound.

Assignment can create shares that remain exposed after the option expires. Earlier expiration is not proof later exposure disappears. Retain all continuing shares/options and explicitly assumed transactions at each relevant date. Compatible protective puts may survive another leg's expiry; ignoring them overstates loss, while double-counting one hedge understates it. Different underlyings, quantities, multipliers or dates can leave residual exposure.

Calendars and diagonals need an interim mark and a continuation plan. At an earlier short-call expiry, a long call calendar's value depends on the later call's remaining value less the short's intrinsic liability. Large rallies do not automatically improve that calendar's economics. Later terminal stock price alone does not determine realized P&L after closes, rolls or assignment. A calculator limited to one valuation date cannot establish unmodeled future trades.

A roll closes one trade and opens another. Its net credit is a current cash flow, not recovery of the original loss. Future obligation, duration, funding and downside may grow. An exit trigger at a future bid/ask must not be combined with today's favorable opposite-side quote to promise a profit. Midpoint or limit execution remains an assumption.

American exercise may occur early; dividends, financing, moneyness and remaining time value affect incentives. Do not promise assignment or nonassignment solely from current moneyness. Event dates and exact expiries must align. Delta is local model sensitivity, not a physical probability of profit, touching a price or assignment. IV, realized volatility, rank and percentile are distinct; the latter require history.
